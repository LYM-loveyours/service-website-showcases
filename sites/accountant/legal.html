01About this example

This site is a design example built by Love Yours Media to show what a Signature-tier website looks like for an accountancy practice. Tallymere does not exist. The people are invented, the client stories are invented, the fees are invented, the address is invented and the phone number comes from the range Ofcom reserves for drama so that it can never reach a real person.

Nothing here is accounting, tax or legal advice. Where the site states a general rule — a filing deadline, a threshold — it says so, states the rule it used and, where the rule could not be confirmed against gov.uk when the page was written, says that too.

The enquiry form posts to an example endpoint with the subject “example enquiry”. On this folder there is no server behind it, so a submission fails rather than pretending to succeed.

02Regulatory position

These four blocks are where a real practice states what it is regulated for and by whom, in words a prospective client can verify on a public register. On this example they are slots.

Professional bodyICAEW / ACCA / AAT — example slot; no membership is claimed.A live site names the body, the firm’s registration and a link to the body’s public register so it can be checked.

Practising certificateExample slot — none issued.Members engaging in public practice in the UK are required to hold a practising certificate. That requirement is why this block exists; nothing is asserted for a fictional firm.

Anti-money-laundering supervisionExample slot — no supervision claimed.An accountancy practice must be supervised for AML purposes, either by a professional body or by HMRC. A live site names the supervisor.

Professional indemnity insuranceExample slot — no cover claimed.A live site states the insurer, the territorial limit and where the policy details can be inspected.

Company detailsExample slot — no company number, VAT number or registered office is claimed.The address used across this example — Second floor, Thornleigh Yard, West Didsbury, Manchester M20 — is invented: no street of that name exists, no postcode is given and no number resolves.

Information Commissioner’s OfficeExample slot — no ICO registration is claimed.Most practices are required to register as a data controller and to publish the registration reference.

No scheme logo, badge artwork, membership number or registration number appears anywhere on this site. That is deliberate: a fictional firm must not display a mark that a real register could contradict.

03Complaints

If something goes wrong, the route on a live practice site would be, in order:

  • Raise it with the partner responsible for your work. Most things are settled in a phone call.
  • If that does not settle it, put it in writing to the other partner. A real practice commits to a response time here — commonly an acknowledgement within five working days and a full reply within a stated period.
  • If you are still not satisfied, the matter goes to the professional body’s complaints process. A live site names that body and links to its complaints page.

On this example there is no partner, no practice and nothing to complain about, so the third step is an example slot rather than a named scheme.

04Privacy notice

What a live version of this site would collect. Your name, email address, telephone number if you give one, and whatever you write in the message field, submitted through the enquiry form. Where you have used the fee estimator or the sample diary, the state of that instrument is included only if you tick the box that says so.

Why. To answer the enquiry you sent. That is the only purpose, and the lawful basis is that answering you is what you asked for.

How long. A live practice states a retention period here — typically enquiries that do not become clients are deleted after a stated number of months, and client records are kept for the period the practice’s professional and legal obligations require.

Your rights. Access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection and portability, and the right to complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

On this example. Nothing is collected. The form has no working endpoint here, there is no analytics script, no advertising pixel, no chat widget and no third-party tag of any kind. The only outbound request the page makes is to Google Fonts for three typefaces, and the imported build self-hosts those so that request disappears.

05Terms of business

A live practice would summarise here what the engagement letter says in full: the work covered, the fee and when it is charged, what happens if records arrive late, ownership of papers, limitation of liability, and how either side ends the arrangement.

Two points from this example’s own published position are worth repeating: the fee is agreed before any engagement letter is signed, and any additional work is priced in writing before it starts.

Fees shown anywhere on this site are illustrative figures for a fictional practice. They are not an offer, a quotation or an indication of what any real firm charges.

06Software and platforms

A practice usually names the accounting platforms it works with. On this example those are text slots with no badge artwork, no partner-status claim and no login link.

Accounting platformsXero / QuickBooks / FreeAgent — example slots.No partner or certification status is claimed and no platform logo appears.

MTD-compatible softwareExample slot.HMRC publishes a list of compatible software. A live site would point at that list rather than at a badge.

Client portalNot present on this example.A live build links to the practice’s own portal provider. Because a fictional firm has no portal, there is no login link anywhere on this site rather than a link that goes nowhere.

07Accessibility

This example was built to be operable by keyboard throughout, including the fee estimator, the sample diary, the deadline calendar and the switching timeline. Computed results are announced through live regions. Colour contrast for body and supporting text exceeds 4.5:1 against both light surfaces used.

Two limitations are stated rather than hidden: the site has not been through a full assistive-technology audit with a screen-reader user, and it has not been tested by anybody using it on their own phone unaided. Both belong to the next stage of work, and neither is claimed as passed.

If something here is hard to use, that is worth knowing: on a live site this paragraph carries an email address and a commitment to reply.
