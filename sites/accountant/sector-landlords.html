Should the next property go in a company?

The short answer is sometimes, and the long answer needs your other income, how long you intend to hold, and whether you need the rent to live on. It is the first of eight things landlords ring us about — answered below the way we would answer them on the phone, including the ones where the answer is that you do not need us.

General information about how the rules work, not advice on your situation. Where a rule has moved recently, it says so.

Q01Should the next property go in a company?

Sometimes. A company pays Corporation Tax on rental profit and gets full relief for mortgage interest as a business expense; an individual landlord gets a basic-rate tax reduction instead. Against that, getting money out of a company costs tax again, and lenders price company mortgages differently.

The honest answer depends on your other income, how long you intend to hold, and whether you need the rent to live on. We work it both ways with your actual numbers before you offer on anything.

We do not advise on the mortgage. That is a broker’s job, and the tax answer changes if the lending does.

Q02What can I actually deduct?

Letting agent fees, repairs that restore rather than improve, insurance, ground rent and service charges, safety certificates, and the cost of getting a property re-let. Replacement of domestic items relief covers a like-for-like sofa or fridge in a furnished let.

Improvements are not deductible against rent — they go against the eventual capital gain instead. A new kitchen of the same standard is usually a repair; an extension is not.

Q03Does Making Tax Digital apply to me?

It depends on qualifying income, and it arrives in stages. From 6 April 2026 for those with qualifying income over £50,000, measured on the 2024–25 return; over £30,000 from April 2027; over £20,000 from April 2028.

If it applies, you keep digital records and send quarterly updates instead of one annual return. We check where you sit and tell you which year you are caught, if any.

Thresholds are HMRC’s published mandation dates. The quarterly update dates used on this site are 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May — check current HMRC guidance before you rely on them.

Q04I have one flat and a job. Is that even worth an accountant?

Often not, and we will say so. One property, PAYE income and no complications is a return you can file yourself in an evening.

It becomes worth paying for at about the point you have a second property, a period of vacancy, a refinance, or a year where you sold something.

Q05What happens when I sell?

A residential property disposal has its own reporting deadline and its own payment on account of Capital Gains Tax, separate from the annual return. It is the single most commonly missed date in property.

Tell us before you exchange, not after you complete. The relief position — whether it was ever your home, whether it was let while you lived elsewhere — is decided by facts we need in advance.

Reporting windows for residential disposals have changed more than once. We will confirm the current window against HMRC guidance at the time, not from memory.

Q06Furnished holiday lets?

The treatment has changed and the old advantages are not what they were. If you have one, the question now is whether the structure you built around it still makes sense.

We will look at it once, properly, rather than repeating a plan that was designed for a different rulebook.

Q07Joint ownership with my spouse — who declares what?

For a jointly held property, income is normally split in line with beneficial ownership, and married couples are treated as 50:50 unless a valid election says otherwise and the underlying ownership matches.

Getting this wrong is common and expensive to unwind. It is a twenty-minute conversation and, sometimes, a form.

Q08I am two years behind. Will you take me on?

Yes. Most of the landlords who call us are behind, not ahead. We quote the catch-up as a one-off before we start, separately from the monthly fee, so you can see what clearing it costs.

Nobody here is going to make you feel worse about it than you already do.

Ninth question?

Bring it to a twenty-minute call. If the answer is that you do not need an accountant yet, that is what you will hear.
