The schedule

Numbered in the order the work falls in a year, not in order of what we would rather sell you.

01

Year-end accounts and Corporation Tax

Statutory accounts for Companies House, the CT600 for HMRC, and a meeting before either is filed. You see the figures and the tax number together, with time to ask why.

For limited companies. Sole traders want line 04.
  • Statutory accounts prepared from your records
  • Corporation Tax computation and CT600
  • Filing at Companies House and HMRC
  • A meeting to go through the accounts before filing
  • Directors’ dividend and salary position for the year
Cadence & feeAnnually£18500from, per month ex VATSee the tariff

02

Bookkeeping, VAT and the quarter

Either you keep the records and we check them, or we keep them. Either way the VAT return is prepared from a reconciled ledger, not from a shoebox in the week it is due.

Companies and sole traders above and below the VAT threshold.
  • Bank reconciliation each month
  • Purchase and sales ledgers kept current
  • Quarterly VAT return prepared and filed
  • The domestic reverse charge, where the trade needs it
  • A short note each quarter on what the numbers show
Cadence & feeQuarterly+ £3500VAT returns, per month ex VATHow this runs for a trade

03

Payroll and the PAYE scheme

Monthly payroll, payslips out on the day you choose, and the RTI submissions HMRC expects. Auto-enrolment assessed each month rather than remembered each year.

Employers with roughly one to twenty people.
  • Monthly payroll run and payslips
  • Real Time Information submissions
  • Pension auto-enrolment assessment and upload
  • Starters, leavers, P45s and P60s
  • P11D and P11D(b) where benefits are provided
Cadence & feeMonthly+ £3000payroll, per month ex VATWhat payroll adds

04

Self Assessment

One return, done in the autumn rather than the last fortnight of January. If a payment on account is coming, you hear the number in October, not the night before.

Directors, sole traders, landlords and anybody HMRC has asked.
  • The return prepared and filed online
  • Balancing payment and payments on account calculated
  • Property, dividend, employment and pension income
  • A note of what to set aside and when
  • The paper-return date flagged if you would rather file that way
Cadence & feeAnnually£7500from, per month ex VATThe dates it hangs on

05

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

Quarterly updates, digital records, and a straight answer about whether the rules apply to you yet. They begin with the highest qualifying incomes and step down over the following two years.

Sole traders and landlords inside the thresholds.
  • A check of whether and when you are caught
  • Digital records set up in software you can live with
  • Quarterly updates prepared and submitted
  • The year-end position pulled together at the end
  • Software text slot — example only; no partner status claimed
Cadence & feeQuarterlywithin the base feeRead the thresholds

06

Company formation and the first year

Incorporation, the first PAYE and VAT registrations if you need them, and the boring set-up that decides how easy the next four years are: year end, share structure, who is a director.

People about to start, and sole traders considering a company.
  • Incorporation and the statutory registers
  • Choosing a year end that suits your trade
  • PAYE, VAT and Corporation Tax registrations
  • A first-year cash and tax timetable
  • The sole-trader-versus-company comparison, with numbers
Cadence & feeOne-offquoted before we startTalk it through

07

Tax planning, once a year, on purpose

A booked hour before the year end, not an afterthought in month eleven. Dividends, pension, capital allowances, whether a second property should sit where it currently sits.

Existing clients, roughly two months before the year end.
  • Profit extraction: salary, dividend, pension
  • Capital allowances on vehicles, plant and tools
  • Timing of purchases either side of the year end
  • Property ownership questions, with a note of what needs a solicitor
  • A written summary of what was decided and why

Work we send elsewhere.

  • AuditWe are not a registered auditor. If a company grows into an audit, we prepare the accounts and work alongside the auditor you appoint.
  • InvestmentsPensions, protection and investment advice need a regulated adviser. We will talk about the tax of a pension contribution; we will not tell you which pension.
  • InternationalOverseas subsidiaries, transfer pricing and non-resident structures go to a firm that does them every week.
  • Court and disputesExpert witness work, forensic accounting and formal insolvency. We will make the introduction.
