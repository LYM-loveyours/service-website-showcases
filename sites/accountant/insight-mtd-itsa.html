Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: does it catch you, and when?

It arrives in three steps, by income, starting with the largest. Most people we speak to are either already in it or two years away, and almost nobody is where they assume they are.

General information about how the rules work, not advice about your position. Check current HMRC guidance before you act on any date here.

Making Tax Digital for Income TaxFolio 33

One annual return becomes four quarterly updates plus a year-end submission. The tax you pay does not change. What changes is how often HMRC hears from you.

It applies to sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment with self-employment or property income. It does not apply to limited companies, and it is separate from Making Tax Digital for VAT, which has been running for years.

Whether you are in it depends on your qualifying income — broadly, your gross self-employment and property income before expenses, not your profit. The threshold steps down over three years, and each step is measured on a return you have already filed.

Source: HMRC guidance on checking eligibility for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, as read on 6 September 2026. HMRC has said it will check qualifying income against each year’s Self Assessment return, so a year of higher income can pull you in even if the following year is quieter. Check current HMRC guidance.

What actually changes

  • You keep digital records of business and property income and expenses — a spreadsheet alone is not enough unless it connects to compatible software.
  • You send a quarterly update to HMRC summarising income and expenses for the period.
  • At the end of the year you make a final submission that pulls the quarters together with anything else — employment, dividends, reliefs.
  • Payment dates do not change. 31 January and, where payments on account apply, 31 July.

The quarterly dates

The quarters run 6 April to 5 July, 6 July to 5 October, 6 October to 5 January and 6 January to 5 April, with the update due about a month afterwards. The dates we use on this site are 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May, and they appear on the deadline calendar computed from today.

Stated plainly: those four dates were taken from a published practice calendar and were not confirmed against gov.uk when this page was written. The mandation dates and thresholds in the table above were. Before you diarise the quarterly dates, check current HMRC guidance or ask us to check it for you.

What we would do about it

The work is front-loaded and then it is boring, which is the right shape. In practice it is three things: work out whether and when you are caught, get records into something that can file, and then keep the quarters moving so the year end is a summary rather than an excavation.

If you are a landlord with one flat and a salary, there is a reasonable chance you are below every threshold and none of this touches you. We would rather tell you that than sell you a subscription.

What we do not know yet

Two things, said openly. First, how HMRC will treat somebody who crosses a threshold mid-way through a year in practice rather than in the guidance. Second, whether the software market settles: several products marketed as MTD-ready in 2026 will not exist in 2029. We name software as a text slot on this site rather than a badge for exactly that reason.
