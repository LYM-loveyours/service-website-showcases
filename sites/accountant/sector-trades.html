The year, in order

A 31 March year end is assumed throughout. Yours may differ; the sequence does not.

Aprilthe year turns

The new year end and the first CIS month

For a 31 March year end, April is where the next year starts and the last one closes. We open the new ledger, agree the van and tool purchases you are planning, and file the CIS return for the month to 5 April like every other month.

May – Junerecords, not reconstruction

Last year’s accounts, while the site notes still make sense

We prepare the accounts within two or three months of the year end, not eleven. Retentions held on completed contracts, work in progress at 31 March and materials on site are the three lines that usually need a conversation rather than a spreadsheet.

5 Julybenefits

P11D, if the company provided anything

Fuel, a company van used privately, private medical cover. If none of it applies, we say so and file nothing. The rule: P11D and P11D(b) are due 6 July after the tax year ends on 5 April.

AugustVAT quarter

The domestic reverse charge, checked contract by contract

Where you invoice another contractor for construction services, the reverse charge usually applies and the VAT is not yours to collect. Where you invoice an end user — a homeowner, a landlord who is not a contractor — it usually is. We check the mix each quarter rather than assuming last quarter’s answer.

Octoberthe halfway conversation

Where the year is going, while you can still change it

A one-hour look at profit to date, the tax that implies, and the plant or vehicle decisions that sit either side of the year end. This is the meeting that most often changes a number.

Decemberfiling

Accounts at Companies House

Nine months after a 31 March year end. Filed well before the deadline, because a late filing penalty is the cheapest avoidable cost in the business.

1 Januarythe tax

Corporation Tax paid

Nine months and one day after the period end. You will have had the figure since June, and the amount to set aside since October.

31 Januarypersonal

Directors’ Self Assessment, filed in November

We aim to have director returns done by the end of November so January is a payment date rather than a scramble. Payments on account, where they apply, fall on 31 January and 31 July.

General rules, stated so you can check them: CIS returns are monthly; the VAT return is due one month and seven days after the quarter end; Companies House accounts nine months after the year end; Corporation Tax nine months and one day after the period end; the CT600 twelve months after. Dates for your own year end are computed on the deadline page. Check current HMRC guidance before acting on any of it.

What we ask you forFolio 27

What we ask you for

Four things, once a month, in whatever form you already keep them.

Not a portal login. Photographs are fine.

  • BankA feed if your bank offers one, a statement if it does not. We reconcile it; you do not.
  • PurchasesMerchant invoices and fuel. Photograph them at the van door on the day — the paper always loses.
  • SubcontractorsNames, UTRs and what you paid. Verification is ours to do, but we cannot verify somebody we do not know about.
  • JobsWhat is finished, what is retained and what is still on site at the month end. Two lines is enough.

Your year, not this one.

Different year end, different mix of contract and domestic work, a retention that has been outstanding for two years. Twenty minutes is enough to say what changes.
