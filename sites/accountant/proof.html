Three worked notesFolio 31

Two years behind, and afraid to open the post

Story 01 · illustrative

What arrived

A joinery company with two directors had not filed accounts for two years. Companies House had issued penalties, the Corporation Tax position was unknown and neither director had filed a personal return since the company started.

They rang in July, having been told by somebody else that the company would be struck off.

What we did

We quoted the catch-up as a single figure before starting, so the cost was known on day one. Two sets of accounts and two CT600s were prepared from bank data and merchant statements, the directors’ returns were filed together, and a time-to-pay arrangement was agreed with HMRC for the tax.

The company now files eight weeks after its year end. The catch-up fee was invoiced once and has not recurred.

A VAT return that never agreed to the bank

Story 02 · illustrative

What arrived

An electrical contractor invoicing both main contractors and homeowners had been treating every invoice the same way. The quarterly return was reconstructed each time from a spreadsheet and never quite matched the bank.

The concern was not the arithmetic. It was that nobody could say which answer was right.

What we did

We separated the contract work from the domestic work and applied the domestic reverse charge to the contract invoices only, invoice by invoice. The bookkeeping moved onto a bank feed, so the quarter reconciles as it goes rather than in the week it is due.

The first correct return took a fortnight. Every one since has taken an afternoon.

A second flat, and the wrong question

Story 03 · illustrative

What arrived

A landlord with one flat and a full-time job had been told by a friend to buy the next one through a company. They came in wanting the company set up.

They had not been told what getting the money back out would cost, or that their lender priced company lending differently.

What we did

We worked it both ways against their actual income, held for five years and held for fifteen, and showed the two totals side by side. On their numbers, personal ownership was better over the shorter hold and the company was better over the longer one.

They bought personally, and we agreed to look again at property four. No company was formed, and the fee for the exercise was one hour.
