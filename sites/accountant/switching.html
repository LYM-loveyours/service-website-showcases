Three steps · about a fortnightFolio 35–36

Changing accountant is a handover, not a fresh start.

The part people dread — telling the current accountant — is the part you do not do. We write to them. It is called professional clearance and it is completely routine.

Put a start date in below and the three steps are worked out from it, in working days. Illustrative: nothing on this page contacts anybody.

The three steps, datedFolio 35

The three steps, dated

Put a date in and the timeline below is worked out from it in working days.

Worked from today.

01

You decide, and we write it down

A twenty-minute call, a fee agreed out loud, then an engagement letter that says exactly what we will do and what it costs. You also give us authority to speak to your current accountant and to HMRC.

  • Nothing is signed until the fee is agreed
  • Anti-money-laundering identity checks happen here — passport or driving licence, and proof of address
  • You tell your current accountant only if you want to; most people prefer us to write first

Step one—Waiting for a start date.

02

We write to your accountant

The professional clearance letter asks two things: whether there is any professional reason we should not act, and for the records we need — last accounts, tax computations, capital allowance pools, payroll data, and the losses or balances carried forward.

  • This is a normal courtesy between practices, not a confrontation
  • A firm may hold papers where fees are outstanding; that is between you and them, and we will tell you if it comes up
  • You do not have to explain yourself to anybody

Step two—Waiting for a start date.

03

Records arrive and we take the deadlines on

We reconcile what arrives against what should exist, register as your agent with HMRC, and confirm in writing which deadline is the first one we own. From that date, chasing it is our job.

  • You get a written list of what is outstanding, if anything is
  • Software is set up or taken over — you keep your own data either way
  • If your previous accountant does not reply, we go ahead from the records you hold and tell you what is missing

What nobody puts on a switching pageFolio 36

The bits nobody puts on a switching page.

  • TimingMid-year is fine. The one time to wait is the fortnight before a filing deadline — let the current firm finish that one, then move.
  • CostThere is no switching fee here and no free months. If catch-up work is needed it is quoted separately before it starts, so you can see it on its own.
  • Your dataIt is yours. If you leave us later we will write the clearance letter to the next practice with exactly the same courtesy.
  • If it stallsSome firms reply the same week and some take a month. We tell you where it is rather than going quiet.
