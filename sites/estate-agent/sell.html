What a valuation visit actually is

Forty-five minutes at the house, usually. We measure the rooms, look at what has genuinely completed within a few streets over the last six months, and leave you a range, the comparable homes it came from, and the fee in writing. Nothing is instructed, photographed or advertised until you say so.

If you want a rough figure before anyone visits, the panel opposite does the arithmetic. It multiplies floor area by a published price per square foot for the neighbourhood. It is not a market appraisal, and it has no idea what your kitchen looks like.

Fees and redress Every instruction comes with written terms of business showing the fee, the term and the notice period. The redress scheme and complaints route for this example agency are set out on the legal page.

The questions we actually get

Do I have to sign a long tie-in?

Eight weeks from the day the home goes live, then two weeks' notice at any time. If you decide not to sell there is nothing to pay, and the photographs and plan are yours.

What if another agent gives me a higher number?

Ask both of us for the completed sales behind it. A figure with no evidence attached is a marketing budget, not a valuation — and an over-priced home usually sells for less after a reduction than it would have at the right number in week one.

Who will I actually be dealing with?

The valuer who visits stays with the sale, does the viewings and rings you with the feedback. The five people who would handle it are named, with direct lines, on the about page.

Where would my home be advertised?

On a real instruction: the agency's own applicant list first, then the portals it subscribes to. On this example site portal names appear as text slots only — no portal relationship is claimed and no listing exists.

Can you sell and let the same property?

Yes, and it is worth costing both before you decide. The letting page sets out the management tiers, landlord fees and the tenant permitted payments in full.
