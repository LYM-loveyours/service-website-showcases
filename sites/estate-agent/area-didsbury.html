Getting into town

The Metrolink line south and the bus corridor along Wilmslow Road do most of the work. Most of the streets in this guide are a fifteen-minute walk from one or the other, and that walk is the single clearest thing separating two otherwise identical houses on price.

Buyers underestimate it in July and reprice it in January. If you are viewing, do the walk once in the dark before you offer.

Schools, and what we will not tell you

Families move here for the schools and then argue about the catchments. We will tell you which schools are near a house and where the admissions criteria are published.

We will never tell you a house is "in catchment". Catchments move, sibling rules override distance, and the only authority is the council's admissions team in the year you apply. An agent who promises you a place is promising something they do not control.

Green edges and water

The gardens and the meadows at the river edge are the reason people put up with the parking. Streets backing onto them carry a premium that is genuinely there and does not appear in any calculation based on floor area.

The flip side is the flood mapping at the very bottom of the district. It is public, it is checkable, and a real listing states it in the material information rather than waiting for the survey.

The village, and one street back

Wilmslow Road carries the shops, the restaurants and the traffic. One street back it is quiet enough to hear the trains. The gap between those two experiences is about ninety seconds' walk and roughly forty thousand pounds.

Buyers who view on a Sunday morning and offer on a Sunday afternoon are the ones who ring us in March about the noise.

Parking, permits and skips

Some roads near the tram stops and the village operate residents' schemes; others do not, and the boundary is not where you would guess. Check the council's current scheme for the exact address before you assume a space, especially if you are buying a flat without an allocated bay.

What sells, and when

Family houses launch best in the last week of February and the first fortnight of September. Flats do not care. A four-bedroom house launched in the third week of December will still sell, but it will sell to whoever is left rather than to whoever wanted it most.

What the houses actually are

Edwardian and Victorian semis

Bay-fronted, three or four bedrooms, 1,200 to 1,600 sq ft. The bulk of the family market and the reason a per-square-foot band holds at all here.

Detached villas

High ceilings, deep plots, 2,000 sq ft and up. Beautiful, expensive to heat, and usually an EPC D or E until somebody spends properly on the fabric.

1930s semis

The wider roads. Longer gardens, garages nobody has cleared, and the easiest houses in the district to extend without a fight.

Flats and conversions

Purpose-built blocks near the tram and converted houses closer to the village. Leasehold: check the unexpired term, the service charge and the ground rent before you offer, not after.
